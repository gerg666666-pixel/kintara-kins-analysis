## Kintara: an MMO where your wallet is the account, and 1,000 $KINS is the gate

Most blockchain games bolt a token onto a game that already works. Kintara does the opposite: the token sits at the exact point where a new player decides whether to stay.

Kintara is a browser-based isometric MMO on Solana. There is no client to install and no account to create — you connect a Solana wallet. The wallet address *is* the account. No email, no password, no separate registration step. You spawn on the Mainland with no tools and no gold, and a tutorial NPC hands you an axe, a pickaxe, a fishing rod and a hammer.

### What is actually live today

The game runs a full gather → craft → sell loop across seven realms. The Mainland is the hub: bank, shops, a healing fountain, the tutorial, and portals out. Whisperwood is the peaceful gathering zone and the only place you may place your one personal shack. The Pond is a 40×40 fishing realm with the communal Roast Pit, the only place raw fish can be cooked for cooking XP. The Wilderness, Wilderness North and Wilderness East are PvP zones where dying can drop your carried items into a tombstone. The Arena is a separate PvP scene.

Five skills level as you play — combat, woodcutting, mining, fishing, cooking. The bank holds 48 slots. The Marketplace opens from the HUD anywhere and supports gold-for-`$KINS` listings.

### The gate, stated plainly

Free play stops at total level 10. That is not a trial timer — progress, skills, quests, items and gold are all kept. But free accounts cannot trade, cannot sell on the Marketplace, cannot deposit to a guild bank, and drop no tombstone on death; carried items are simply lost.

Holding **1,000 $KINS** in the connected wallet opens four more realms: The Shores, The Dunes, The Emberstone and The Venomweaver. Keeping that balance for **24 hours** unlocks trading, Marketplace selling, guild-bank deposits and tombstone drops.

That two-stage design — instant realm access, delayed economic access — is the most interesting thing in its architecture: it separates wanting to see more world from wanting to participate in the economy.

### Where the token goes

The guide documents two `$KINS` sinks, and both are unusually specific. A paid spinner spin costs $5 USD worth of `$KINS` at the live price; 50% is burned and 50% goes to the game treasury. A Marketplace gold-for-`$KINS` sale splits 95% to the seller and 5% to the treasury. There is also a free spin every 24 hours with the same prize table, so the wheel is not paywalled. Cosmetics are bought with gold, not `$KINS`.

**One promising aspect:** the sinks are tied to actions players already take, not to a separate staking screen. Burning half of every paid spin is a real supply reduction attached to a real in-game impulse.

**One unanswered question:** the public guide documents utility and sinks but publishes no supply schedule, no emission curve, and no distribution breakdown — and no dated roadmap or changelog. Where new `$KINS` comes from, and at what rate, is not stated anywhere I could verify. That is the number I would want before forming any view.
